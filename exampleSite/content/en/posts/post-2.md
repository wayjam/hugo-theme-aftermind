The handover ledger reaches page 112, followed by blank ruled sheets. I took off my old badge and set it on the paper; the lanyard edges have frayed.

I used to think every job ended with a proper handover: reading passwords, schedules, and edge cases aloud while the next person nodded, took notes, and let you walk out with a clean conscience.{{< note >}}In reality, whatever took over did not need to hear you read anything aloud. It ingested five years of email threads in half a second.{{< /note >}}

Now there is more time than anyone knows how to spend. I have started logging small, inconsequential observations here—a new scratch on the elevator mirror, or the convenience store switching to a different oden broth. These notes have no audience and no KPI. Writing them is only a quiet way of proving that, in the gaps between perfect machine output, someone is still entertaining themselves slowly and with typos.
