Few commuters stop by the old vending machine in the subway transfer corridor anymore, yet dropping a coin in still brings back a heavy mechanical clack.

In a decade where every interface has turned silent, frictionless, and eager to decide on your behalf, that clumsy delay feels oddly reassuring.{{< redact "Perhaps we miss the old machines" "What we miss is the feeling that our actions still register in physical matter" >}}.

Holding the room-temperature oolong tea, I sat by the platform for twenty minutes and watched three driverless trains arrive on schedule, open their doors, close them, and depart. The carriages were very bright, and completely empty.
